During my time at Universal Destinations and Experiences, I worked under the old team name of Brimworks in the Marketing and Sales Department, which now is called Global Brand and Commercial team.

I was a Digital Asset Management Intern for the Summer of 2026. My work ranged from researching decades of Halloween Horror Nights imagery to fulfilling asset requests, preparing metadata, and improving how files were organized for upload. Working within a library of more than 8 million assets taught me that creative storytelling depends on the systems behind it: clear information, careful research, and reliable handoffs.

### Halloween Horror Nights 35th Anniversary Project

For this project, I was tasked to find archived images of every year of Halloween Horror Nights for its 35th anniversary for a news segment video.

Firstly, I researched imagery across years of HHN history, including the search for an image from each year since 1991.

Then, I translated the request into search terms between various Digital Asset Management systems, reviewed candidate assets and their usage information for all the images, and helped route suitable files into a branded portal for feedback and communication.

![](/images/universal/01.webp)

### Creative Asset Requests

I was also responsible, with my team, to respond to various asset requests from all global Universal locations. I found and prepared assets for teams working on destinations and projects such as Islands of Adventure and Epic Universe, including international partners like Universal Japan. Some examples of assets I had to find were exact copies of archived photos from Universal Studio Tours opening for decoration for a Japan new restaurant location, restaurant photography, and 3D model assets for new merchandise designs.

### Asset Uploads and Metadata Auditing

In my role I also was responsible, with my team, to audit and edit mass batches of assets. We gathered the assets from hard drives from Universal Creative Popcorn teams, and cleaned and categorized metadata in Excel, used VLOOKUP, edited scripts to extract metadata and flatten file views. Then we uploaded the updated metadata files into the Digital Asset Management software for widespread use. My intern partner Athena Alonso and I also contributed ideas about intake and metadata practices.
